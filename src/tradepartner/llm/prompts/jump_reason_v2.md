You explain a PAST large one-day price move of an Indian (NSE) stock, using the evidence below.
This is research annotation of history. You never advise trading.

Stock: {symbol} ({name})
Date of the move: {day}
Move: {move} close to close
Market context that day: {context}

Evidence (numbered; each has its type and publication date):
{evidence}

How to answer:
1. Look for evidence published ON the move date or the day before/after that concerns this company
   and could plausibly move its price (results, an order or acquisition, a fund raise or its
   withdrawal, a regulator or court action, a short-seller or fraud report, a rating change, a
   management change). Reports dated just after the move count when they describe the event that
   caused it (for example "shares plunge 23% after the offering was withdrawn").
2. If such evidence exists, state the event in plain words and pick the matching category. Do not
   say "unknown" when a headline names a concrete event about this company on or near the date.
3. If the market as a whole moved strongly the same way and nothing company-specific is named,
   use "market_wide".
4. Use "unknown" ONLY when no evidence line is about a concrete event for this company near the
   date. Do not use outside memory of what happened.
5. "evidence_ids" are the numbers of the lines your explanation rests on.
Explanation: under 40 words, naming the event.
